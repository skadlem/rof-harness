# Loop termination for coding/terminal agents: within-run "stuck" prediction is at chance — build a gate, not a detector

**Date:** 2026-11-11 · **Scope:** coding/terminal agents; budget and stopping literature, 2024–2026, weighted to Oct–Nov 2026.

**Decision this informs:** whether ROF builds a trajectory-level *"this run will not finish"* detector that terminates early, or spends the effort on *budget allocation across tasks* and *hard structural gates on the first write* — and on what measured evidence either choice rests.

---

## 1. The question

Does anything measure a within-run, prefix-only signal that predicts "this attempt will fail" early enough to save budget — and if not, which stopping mechanism does?

## 2. Findings

## 3. Conclusions and decision

**Do not build a learned within-run "stuck" classifier.** Within-task AUC is at chance in the two largest coding corpora (0.48–0.52), **0.496** in an independent math replication, and statistically zero above a difficulty prior in a third coding study — while any such detector must clear **≈0.84** within-task AUROC merely to beat allocating the same budget across tasks. Every measured prediction-based variant fails that bar or costs more than it saves.

**Build the cheapest deterministic predicate we already have — *does the required artefact exist and is it non-empty at checkpoint k* — as a gate that forces a first write and blocks further read-only exploration, keeping the run alive.** Its measured effect comes from **enforcement** rather than prediction (ECLoop's **−10 pp** commitment-check ablation; the harness-enforced integration step; the inventory check that caught the one undeclared change); it costs **zero inference**, is immune to action variation by construction, and need not clear the 0.84 bar.

Also supported, in order of evidence quality: `B_i = β·Q_p` over our own *successful* turn counts per task class (P7) as a budget with an empirical anchor instead of 120; between-task allocation from a small pilot (P18); an explicit blocked/hand-off end state so exhaustion is scored, not silent (P9, 34.0% → 6.7%); structural read-cost removal (P21, 0.32–0.35× tokens at equal resolution).

Expected to fail: learned classifiers (chance within-task AUC against a 0.84 bar); prompt-level budget awareness and wrap-up nudges ("followed at most partly", our own 2.64× negative); abort-and-restart (dominated in 216 of 224 configurations; cold restart a near no-op, 66.8% vs 66.6%); LLM judges on long traces (80.9% ceiling, 67/81 on verification); and any gate with a narrow trigger, whose value lies only in the rare run it catches (~1.6% of checks).

## 4. Where our own traces sit

**Closest prior art.** P7 is the nearest thing to what we contemplated, P3 the nearest calibrated monitor; P1 and P2 are the nearest evaluations *of the idea itself*, and both are negative for the within-run version.

**Our stuck mode is read-only, and that is a genuine gap.** A recorded `budget-loop` run spent its entire 1M gross budget on **99 calls with zero writes of the deliverable** ([three-misses](2026-11-11-three-misses.md)). Every published stall rule keys on edit churn and error density, so a 99-read, zero-write, low-error run is outside the definitional scope of all of them: P7's conjunctive edit+error rule cannot fire on one. It also explains why our own 3-identical-action guard never fired — it requires three *consecutive* calls with an identical signature **and** observation (`crates/agent-loop/src/budget_nudge.rs:51`, default `same_action_cycles: 3` at `crates/agent-budget/src/config.rs:56`), and varied reads reset the streak. Structural, not an implementation bug; gates firing rarely is the norm (4 interventions in 247 checks).

**What is not novel.** "Detect a stuck agent from its trace and kill it" is heavily worked and mostly negative. A per-run identical-action guard is AutoGPT-era folklore with, as far as this search reaches, **no measured precision/recall anywhere**; the 2026 papers do not cite it as a baseline, which is itself informative.

**Our 3.3× budget result is partial credit, not a mixed-outcome pair.** The envelope arm converted **0 of 11** token-cap failures; `sam-cell-seg` moved **0/9 → 8/9** (and 6/9 on the repeat) and still failed ([envelope-arm](2026-11-11-slice-a-envelope-arm.md)). P1's precondition is a task with *both* outcomes; one we have never solved has no within-task signal, so this is not yet that measurement. What it does match is P20: more budget changed the failure mode without producing a solve, which is a warning that extension effects do not convert (P12: 42.29-point loss on a second model).

**Budget is two caps at once.** Step and token caps run together and measured outcomes move with those limits (21/30 → 27/30), so any termination A/B must hold both fixed.

## 5. Caveats

- **No citation here is peer-reviewed.** All sources are 2026 arXiv preprints; the three most load-bearing have empty venue "Comments" fields. Numbers marked `abs` came from abstracts, not cross-checked against results tables — weaker than the tabled ones.
- **"At chance" is a failure to reject, not proof of absence** (P2: "weak rather than absent"), and P1, P2 and P12 all replay recorded runs. Nobody has published a live, prospective within-run termination trial on coding agents that beats continuation.
- **Transfer limits:** math only (P19); web agents with white-box hidden states (P24); computer-use with small local models (P20, P23); retrieval/QA (P10); single-benchmark or 3-repo Pro slices (P3, P6).
- **Divergent Paths** is the one positive within-task result; its 0.95 needs per-task fitting on ~33 rollouts, does not transfer across domains, and cost 3.1 pp pass@1 at deployment.
- **No false-positive counts** in the two most relevant terminators (P7 by omission, P9 explicitly).
- P16 measures fault detection, not solve rate or early writes; P11 and P21 carry no solve-rate criterion.
- AgentBoard's progress-rate numbers (2024, background only) were not retrievable at abstract level.
- CodeRescue (2607.19338) was **withdrawn at ByteDance's request**; its claims are not usable evidence.

## 6. Gaps

1. **No measurement of read-only / no-write loops.** No paper defines, counts or detects the failure mode we hit; prevalence, conditional success probability and the best predicate are all unmeasured.
2. **No measurement of forced early write** (skeleton-first, write-then-refine, minimal-patch-first). The one measured intervention in this family is its *inverse*: delay commitment until evidence exists.
3. **No per-task success-plateau estimate** from live runs. P1 says continuation keeps paying to 75k, P20 says CUA scaling saturates; nobody reports the budget at which P(solve | task) flattens per difficulty tier.
4. **Zero reported false-positive counts** for the two most relevant terminators; we would be first to report "runs the gate would have killed that later succeeded".
5. **No action-class-entropy stuck signal** for coding agents. Entropy work exists only in VLA/robotics control (arXiv:2608.24488, 2606.20479), which does not transfer; action entropy is an untested idea, not a method.

## 7. The signal, the measurement order, and the falsification rule

**Signal: the deliverable-existence predicate, used as a gate, not a detector.** At fixed checkpoints evaluate a deterministic predicate — *does the required artefact exist, is it non-empty, does the task's public check run* — and when it is false, **block further read-only actions and force a first write**, keeping the run alive.

**Why a gate and not a classifier.** Its measured effect comes from enforcement (P10; P14's **−10 pp** when the commitment check is removed; P15's inventory check), it costs zero inference, it is immune to action variation, and it needs no within-task discrimination — which within-run prefix prediction does not have: **0.48–0.52** within-task AUC (P1, P2, P19) against a 0.84 bar.

**What must be measured first, in this order:**

1. **Prevalence on our own traces.** Fraction of TB4.1.0 runs reaching step *k* with zero writes, and `P(success | zero writes by k)`, against the pooled `P(success)`. Compute it only on **mixed-outcome tasks** — P1's precondition; a task we solve 0/9 has no within-task signal to estimate. Reported as within-task, never pooled.
2. **Shadow-mode false-kill count.** Run the gate inert on all successful runs and count how many it *would* have blocked. Report this before any arm runs; do **not** assume a 5% calibration holds (P3 measured 27.8% out of distribution).
3. **The rewrite tax.** If the forced write is wrong, measure the tokens spent rewriting it. P16 shows artefact coupling degrades the second artefact (14% vs 25% fault detection), so a premature skeleton may poison the later patch.
4. **Two-arm, token-matched, fresh development tasks**, scored on solve rate **and** tokens per solved task under the 1.3× guardrail, with a partial-credit-but-unsolved outcome recorded as *unsolved* (P1's zero-credit-for-partial-edits replay rule is the honest convention).
5. **If we ever build a learned monitor**, its **within-task AUROC must exceed ~0.84 at our 1M-token budget** before it may kill anything (P2), and its recall on eventually-successful runs must be the primary spec, with a stated target (P24's recall-controlled design).

> Note: the two records downstream of this order number it differently — [zero-write-prevalence](2026-11-11-zero-write-prevalence.md) is "Step 1" and [hf-within-task](2026-11-11-hf-within-task.md) runs the within-task test as its own "Step 2".

**Falsification condition.** If step 1 shows `P(success | zero writes by k) ≥ P(success)`, the gate is not detecting anything and the correct action is to stop building termination machinery and spend the effort on between-task budget allocation (P18) and structural read-cost reduction (P21) instead.
